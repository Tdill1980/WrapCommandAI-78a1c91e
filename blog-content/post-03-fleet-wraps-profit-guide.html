Fleet Pricing Strategy: Win the Job AND Keep Your Margins

Here is where shops make their biggest mistake with fleet work. They think "fleet" means "massive discount." They slash their per-vehicle price to "win the deal" and end up working for peanuts on a massive project that consumes their entire month.

Do not do that.

Fleet work already has built-in efficiencies that lower your real cost per vehicle. You do not need to give those savings to the customer. You keep them.

Why Fleet Jobs Are Already Cheaper for You

  • One design, many vehicles: Your $500 design investment is spread across the fleet. At 10 vehicles, that is $50 each.
  • Learning curve gains: Every subsequent install is faster. Vehicle five takes 20-30% less time than vehicle one.
  • Reduced project management: One client. One contact. One approval chain. Instead of managing 10 separate retail clients with 10 different opinions.
  • Scheduling efficiency: Vehicles arrive on a set schedule. No chasing appointments. No last-minute reschedules.
  • Material ordering simplicity: Same design. Same vehicle. Same order repeated. Zero custom one-offs.

These efficiencies mean your TRUE cost per vehicle on a fleet job is 10-20% lower than the same job quoted as a one-off retail wrap. But your per-vehicle price stays at or near retail levels.

The fleet efficiency play (10-van example):

 

Quoted cost per vehicle: $3,028 (standard)

Actual cost by vehicle 5+ (efficiency adjusted): ~$2,600

 

Option A: Price at full-cost 50% margin ($6,056/van)

Total: 10 x $6,056 = $60,560

Actual cost (efficiency adjusted avg): ~$27,000

Actual profit: $33,560 (55.4% real margin)

 

Option B: Offer modest fleet "discount" ($5,500/van)

Total: 10 x $5,500 = $55,000

Actual profit: $55,000 - $27,000 = $28,000 (50.9% real margin)

Client feels they got a deal. You still clear $28K.

See how that works? Even with a "discount," you clear $28,000 because the efficiency gains stack in your favor. The client thinks they got a deal at $5,500 versus your retail rate of $6,000+. Your actual margin is over 50% because your costs decrease with volume while your price barely moves.

The numbers are exciting. But they mean nothing if you do not have clients. So let's talk about prospecting. Because fleet clients are not hiding. They are driving past your shop every single day. You just have not been looking for them.

Industries That Consistently Need Fleet Wraps

  • Plumbing and HVAC: 5-30 vehicles typical, high turnover rate, always adding trucks
  • Electrical contractors: 5-50 vans and trucks, often growing rapidly
  • Pest control: 10-100+ vehicles, many are franchises with national branding
  • Landscaping and lawn care: trucks, trailers, and vans, seasonal vehicle additions
  • Real estate brokerages: agent vehicles, office-branded SUVs
  • Food delivery and catering: box trucks and vans
  • Home cleaning services: sedans and small SUVs, fleets of 5-25+
  • Construction companies: trucks, vans, heavy equipment
  • Mobile medical and home health: sedans and SUVs
  • Courier and logistics: cargo vans, sprinters, box trucks

Every single one of those businesses wants their vehicles to look professional and to generate brand impressions. Most of them just have not been approached by someone who can show them the value and make the process easy.

The Prospecting Playbook: 6 Steps to Your First Fleet Deal

1. Build a one-page fleet proposal template. This is your weapon. It should include: before/after vehicle mockups, pricing tiers for 5/10/20 vehicles, your timeline commitment, materials and warranty information, and payment terms. Clean layout. Professional design. Make it impossible to say no.

2. Create AI mockups using their actual branding. This is the game-changer. Use RestylePro AI to preview what their brand looks like on their vehicle type. When a business owner sees their logo, colors, and phone number on a wrapped van for the first time, something clicks in their brain. It becomes real. The conversation shifts from "if" to "when" and "how many." Nothing closes a fleet deal faster than a visual that makes them FEEL the impact.

3. Identify 20 local businesses with 5+ service vehicles. Drive around your area for 30 minutes. Count plain white vans. Count trucks with peeling vinyl or faded magnets. Check commercial parking lots. Search Google Maps satellite view for fleet parking areas. Use LinkedIn to find local business owners. Every unbranded service vehicle you see is a walking, driving, parking lead.

4. Walk in. Do not email. Do not cold call. Walk in. Show up with your proposal and your mockup on a tablet. Ask for the owner or fleet manager. Show them the visualization. Explain the ROI: a wrapped van gets 30,000-70,000 impressions per day. Over a 5-year wrap life, that is 50-125 million impressions per vehicle. Compare that to a billboard at $2,000-$5,000 per month. The wrap pays for itself in months.

5. Offer a pilot vehicle. If the company has 15 trucks and they are hesitant to commit to all 15, propose wrapping 1-2 vehicles first. Let them see the quality on the road. Let their drivers experience the reactions. Let the phone calls from new customers start rolling in. Then close the rest of the fleet. This tactic works because seeing is believing -- and once that first wrapped truck is on the road, the owner feels the brand impact viscerally.

6. Ask about vehicle replacement schedules. This is the long game. Most fleet vehicles get replaced every 3-5 years. When a company buys or leases new vehicles, they need new wraps. Get on that cycle and you have recurring revenue baked into the relationship forever. Ask every fleet client: "When are your next new vehicles coming in?" Put it on your calendar. Send a reminder 30 days before. Be the obvious, easy choice.
