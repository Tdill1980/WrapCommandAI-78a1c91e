When to Choose 3M

3M's wrap film is the film that most installers learn on, and for good reason. It is incredibly forgiving. The Comply adhesive with air egress channels means you can lay it down, lift it up, reposition, and lay it again without trapping air or losing tack.

Best Use Cases for 3M:

  • Full wraps on passenger vehicles with complex body lines. If you are wrapping a late-model car or SUV with deep recesses around door handles, bumper contours, and compound curves at the fenders, 3M gives you the working time and conformability to handle those areas without fighting the film.
  • Luxury and exotic vehicles. When the stakes are high and the curves are aggressive, you want a film that slides into recesses and stays put. 3M excels here.
  • Wraps that need to be removed cleanly. If you are doing a promotional wrap that comes off in 6-12 months, 3M's adhesive technology is hard to beat for clean removal. Lease vehicles and rental fleets are good examples.
  • Jobs where the installer is less experienced. This is not a knock -- it is just reality. 3M is more forgiving of technique imperfections. If you have a newer installer handling a job, 3M reduces the risk of visible errors.

Installer Tips for 3M:

  • Use a medium-firm squeegee (not too hard) -- the film responds well to moderate pressure.
  • Post-heat all edges and recesses to 200-212F for maximum adhesion and long-term hold.
  • On deep recesses, use a heat gun to relax the film before stretching. 3M has excellent memory, so it will conform and stay.
  • For channel areas and complex curves, do not be afraid to lift and reposition. The adhesive is designed for this.
  • Allow 24-48 hours before washing after install for full adhesive bond.

When to Choose Avery Dennison MPI 1105

Avery Dennison MPI 1105 is a workhorse. It is the film you reach for when you need consistency across a high-volume run, excellent print quality, and reliable outdoor performance. The Easy Apply RS adhesive has a slightly different feel from 3M -- a touch more initial grab -- but once you get used to it, it lays down beautifully.

Best Use Cases for Avery:

  • Fleet graphics and commercial vehicles. When you are wrapping 10, 20, or 50 of the same vehicle, Avery's consistency from roll to roll is a major advantage. The print results are uniform and the install behavior is predictable across the entire run.
  • Box trucks, trailers, and flat-panel vehicles. Avery thrives on larger, flatter surfaces. The adhesive has excellent grab on these substrates and the film lays flat with minimal effort.
  • Wall wraps and non-vehicle applications. While WePrintWraps also offers dedicated wall wrap film at $3.25/sq ft, some shops prefer MPI 1105 for wall applications that need to match the same brand graphics on their fleet. It works on painted drywall, glass, and smooth surfaces.
  • Long-term outdoor graphics. For wraps that need to look good for 5-7 years in direct sunlight, Avery MPI 1105 with matched laminate delivers excellent UV resistance and color retention.

Installer Tips for Avery Dennison MPI 1105:

  • The initial tack is slightly higher than 3M, so position carefully on first lay. Use a hinge method on large panels to control placement.
  • Heat is your friend. Avery responds very well to heat -- it becomes more pliable and conformable when warmed up, so do not skip the heat gun on curves.
  • For rivets and corrugations, apply generous heat and use a firm squeegee or rivet brush to work the film into channels. Avery's adhesive grabs and holds well on textured surfaces.
  • Post-heat all edges to at least 200F. This is critical with any film, but Avery especially benefits from thorough post-heating to lock in the edges.
  • For large flat panels, use a wide squeegee and work from center outward. The Easy Apply RS technology lets air escape efficiently on these surfaces.
